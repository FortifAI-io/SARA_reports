Verdict — High (7 / 10), and the shape of it is the point. Zee is not an earnings-manipulation case: the financial-statement core is clean — an unqualified audit, a clean CARO 2020, no restatement, and forensic screens that all read benign (detail in Pillar 5 and Annexure A3). The High rating is set entirely by the governance wrapper around those clean numbers — active regulatory enforcement, a control-without-ownership promoter structure, related-party value-leakage, and a heavy litigation load. On the financials alone Zee would sit in the Low band; it is the governance, related-party and enforcement layer that lifts it to High.

Why 7 and not lower: an enforcement-tier override floors the composite at 7 on the active securities-account freeze plus the live SEBI proceeding; the confirmed-fraud tier does not fire, because those matters remain sub judice. The override sets a floor, not a ceiling — derivation in Annexure A4.

A1Methodology & Sources

This review applies the D7 Governance & Forensic Accounting (GFA) framework — a six-pillar forensic model operationalised for D7 due diligence — adapted for an Indian listed issuer. The composite is the higher of three independent readings: max(weighted blend, Pillar-5 floor, override floor). Pillar 5 (forensic accounting) carries the dominant weight; an override sets a floor, never a ceiling, and forces human review.

India adaptations applied: the auditor’s report & CARO 2020 replace the 10-K/ICFR spine (here the CARO is clean — no fraud reported — which is disconfirming evidence, recorded as such); a standalone-versus-consolidated reconciliation lens is used to surface subsidiary drag and intra-group flows; the Dechow F-Score is treated as directional only, as it is calibrated on US SEC (AAER) enforcement with no Indian equivalent — Indian forums (SEBI adjudication, NCLT/NCLAT, SAT) are the local analogue; litigation is read from Indian forums (no PACER). The generic revenue-integrity assessment is applied (not the retail protocol), centred on whether related-party transactions affect revenue, profit or cash flow.

Sources: Zee Entertainment FY2024-25 Annual Report (Ind AS standalone & consolidated, auditor’s report, CARO, Key Audit Matters, Note 46 related-party disclosures, segment Note 40); D7 CDD report FORT10070 (§5 screening, §6 adverse media, §7 regulatory, §8 litigation), data fetched 19 May 2026. The audited annual filing is the authoritative source for financial-statement data; secondary sources do not override it. Peer benchmarking is not applied in this review.

Does the ownership structure allow inappropriate influence or avenues to conceal/manipulate, and are minorities treated equally? P2 reads share/holding structure, ownership concentration and control, company history, and insider transactions.

2.2 Ownership concentration and control. The defining feature is a promoter holding of 3.99% (3.83 Cr of 96.05 Cr shares) against 96.02% public float. For a founder-controlled media group this is structurally anomalous: the promoter family retains practical control and naming association while holding almost no economic stake — the alignment that normally disciplines a controlling shareholder is largely absent. This is the inverse of the usual Indian promoter-pledge risk (there is little stake left to pledge), but it raises the same governance question: control without proportionate economic exposure.

3.2 Affiliate flows — impact on profit and cash. The single most material item is a ₹93.1 Cr dividend received from subsidiary ATL Media Limited in FY25 (nil in FY24). It is properly disclosed; the point for a reader assessing earnings durability is that a portion of standalone profit is an intra-group distribution rather than third-party operating income.

3.2 Value-leakage events. Two corroborated clusters, flagged in the auditor’s Key Audit Matters: (a) a single ₹80.9 Cr Margo exceptional charge, recognised on an unfavourable arbitration over a guarantee invoked by a customer of subsidiary Margo Networks. Per Note 30 read with Note 32, this ₹809 M provision comprises ₹37.2 Cr of balances written off plus a ₹43.7 Cr provision for diminution in the Margo investment (₹372 M + ₹437 M = ₹809 M) — a single charge, not three separate ones; (b) ₹170.6 Cr of inter-corporate deposits given to related parties, now the subject of arbitration for recovery. A second auditor KAM — recoverability of investment in subsidiaries carried at cost — reinforces the same affiliate-exposure concern. Together these are the documented channel through which affiliate exposure has crystallised into charges and disputes — the forensic substance behind the broader fund-diversion allegations, distinct from those allegations themselves.

3.1 / 3.3 Recurring related-party cost base. Purchase of services from related parties runs ~₹320.6 Cr (ATL Media FZ-LLC ₹146.7 Cr, Asia Today ₹67.5 Cr, DSMCS ₹52.4 Cr, Z5X Global ₹54.8 Cr) plus purchase of media content from Zee Studios (₹71.9 Cr). 5.3 · Overall Revenue Analysis
Revenue trajectory — standalone vs consolidated
₹ Cr; own-trend and sector-market frame (no peer panel in this review)
Revenue is flat-to-declining, against an Indian linear-TV market where advertising fell ~10% and subscription ~8% in 2025 (FICCI-EY, cited in D7 CDD §10). The read is one of revenue durability/structural decline — a forward-reliability concern — not revenue inflation. Internal coherence holds: receivables fell (DSO 75→66d) as revenue softened, the opposite of channel-stuffing.

The top-down screen finds no internal incoherence of the manipulation type: revenue is not rising while cash falls (CFO/NI > 1.0× throughout), receivables are not outrunning sales (DSO declining), and gross margin moved with, not against, the revenue trend. The only divergence is external and benign-directional: revenue below the sector trend, consistent with documented OTT migration and the Reliance–Disney/JioStar competitive shift. This routes to a forward-durability watch, not a forensic finding.

  • SEBI fund-diversion show-cause notice independently corroborated.   • Board composition (FY26 secretarial report): the board is constituted of Non-Executive and Independent Directors only. Punit Goenka was appointed CEO with effect from 18 November 2024 and is described as focused on operational responsibilities, with no Manager in the company — i.e. the founder-family CEO runs operations but does not sit on the board. Part mitigant (CEO separated from the board), part structural oddity (the CEO is not accountable as a director).
  • Confirmations from the secretarial report: no resignation of statutory auditors and no director disqualified under Section 164 during FY26 — both relevant to the P5 read (audit continuity is a methodology override trigger that did not fire) and to P1/P4.
Accounting
  • Content-amortisation policy change (FY26) — the content-NRV watch crystallising. In Q4 FY26 the company moved movie amortisation from straight-line over 60 months to a front-loaded basis (50% in the first two years), taking a ~₹300 Cr charge. The FY25 policy (Note 2) confirms the prior straight-line / five-year basis, which deferred content cost relative to consumption and flattered FY23–FY25; the FY26 change is the correction. This converts the P5 content-NRV watch into a realised charge in the subsequent year.
  • The FY26 secretarial compliance report provides no accounting assurance: it expressly states it has not verified the correctness of the financial records and is neither an audit nor an expression of opinion. It should not be cited as accounting comfort; the accounting flags remain sourced to the FY25 annual report and the FY26 transcript disclosure above.
