Xa Codex Screening Summary · Q1 FY2026

Codex ingested 36 vendor invoices from Holcim GHBS Philippines via OpenText VIM during the Q1 FY2026 period. The four-layer screening engine fired 12 signals across 11 invoices, intercepting USD 727,837 in invoice value before posting to SAP. The most significant finding is a confirmed vendor swap case (INV-2026-02-0044, Concrete Form Supply): the invoice cited a PO that was actually issued to PhilSteel Fabricators for an unrelated steel reinforcement order. This is a textbook payment diversion pattern that three-way match alone would not have caught. The invoice was blocked from MIRO posting and an investigation case opened.

Layer 4 (ERP Verification) produced the highest number of fired signals (6 of 12), validating the architecture decision to prioritise PR-PO-GR-Invoice cross-verification for the VIM-resident profile. Notable Layer 4 findings include a segregation-of-duties failure (ERP-PR-004, Heritage Construction Materials — same person requisitioned and approved the PR), and a 15% price-tolerance breach (ERP-PRICE-001, Steel Forge Industries) where the invoice unit price exceeded the PO unit price beyond the configured 5% tolerance band.

Layer 1 (Document Forensics) caught four manipulation patterns: an incremental PDF update over the invoice amount field (Pacific Coast Trading), an OCR overlay attack hiding a PHP 4M discrepancy between visible and machine-readable amounts (Visayas Mining Supplies), a document hash match across two different vendor invoices (Pacific Alloy Industries reusing Manila Steelcast's invoice forensic fingerprint — consistent with the bidding collusion signature flagged by Tender in the same period), and a white-fill overlay masking content in an invoice line-item region (Lumber Construction Co.).

Four D7 entity verifications were triggered, with one returning Warn (Quickbill Trading Corp — new vendor at 45 days, virtual office, no financial filings). The recent bank change on Northwind Industrial PH (12 days before invoice submission) was held for Treasury review per the VM-BANK-CHANGE-001 protocol. SLA compliance for the period stood at 87%, with two held invoices breaching the 3-business-day SLA and triggering line-manager escalation.

Layer 4 dominance: 6 of 12 signals (50%) come from Layer 4 ERP Verification. Document forensic spread: Layer 1 fired four signals across four different attack types: incremental PDF updates (Pacific Coast Trading), OCR overlay attack (Visayas Mining Supplies), document hash reuse (Pacific Alloy), and white-fill overlay (Lumber Construction). D7 trigger effectiveness: Four D7 triggers fired: 2 from new vendor (Quickbill, Northwind), 1 from high-value invoice (Steel Forge), and 1 from recent bank change (also Northwind). D7 correctly distinguished entity legitimacy from transaction integrity, which is what the conditional D7 trigger logic is designed to do.
