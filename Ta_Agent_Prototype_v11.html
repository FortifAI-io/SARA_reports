How Ta detects trade-diversion risk
Ta reads persisted anomalies from the Anomaly DB and evidences them through a two-pillar composite — the D7 counterparty signal fused with the CCM (SAP) transaction signal. This document sets out the rule catalogue, the data each rule consumes, how signals are scored and tiered, how humans adjudicate, and the system's limits. Detection logic runs in the platform rule layer; Ta consumes its output and does not compute detection on demand.

Change Control
⚖ Scope & boundary. Ta detects behaviors and patterns indicating potential regulatory exposure. It does not assess legal compliance, render legal opinions, or conclude that a violation has or has not occurred. Every anomaly is a case requiring human review; disposition is always made by a qualified person, and legal disposition by counsel.
What Ta does
  • Surfaces persisted anomalies from the Anomaly DB across the trade-control rule set.
  • Fuses two independent signals — D7 counterparty and CCM transaction — into a single explained case.
  • Presents a plain-language rationale, the underlying evidence, and a full audit trail for each anomaly.
  • Routes anomalies through a human triage → escalation → disposition lifecycle.
What Ta does not do
  • Does not decide compliance or illegality — humans and counsel adjudicate.
  • Does not generate item classifications or licence determinations — these are Honeywell-supplied and read only.
  • Does not act on transactions (no block, hold, or release is executed by Ta).
  • Does not transmit source data back to Fortifai servers.
Regulatory frame
The rule set is organised around three US trade-control regimes: EAR (Export Administration Regulations — dual-use, BIS), ITAR (International Traffic in Arms Regulations — defense articles, DDTC), and OFAC (sanctions — SDN and the 50% Affiliates Rule). Each anomaly is tagged to the regime whose exposure it evidences, so the same population can be read through the lens each authority cares about.
Each rule below shows its detection methodology and the data it consumes, split into Honeywell internal data, Honeywell external / reference data, and D7 source data. Detection runs in the platform rule layer; Ta renders the result.
Two-pillar composite score
Every anomaly carries a composite score (0–100) formed from two independent pillars. Neither pillar alone confirms diversion risk; the composite is what a list-only or transaction-only tool cannot produce.
D7 · Counterparty signal
Weighted from screening status (SDN / Entity List / debarred / 50%-rule owner), jurisdiction risk score, ownership resolution confidence, and match-layer strength (exact → alias → jurisdictional).
CCM · Transaction signal
Weighted from item control status (ECCN / USML), licence coverage (present / expired / value-exceeded / missing), destination consistency, and post-confirmation changes (e.g. ship-to redirection).
Score bands
90
Critical — score ≥ 90. Deterministic or near-deterministic exposure; expedite.
75
High — score 75–89. Strong composite; escalate on evidence.
60
Medium — score < 75. Probabilistic; review before escalating.
Tiering model
Tier 1
Deterministic. A definite condition is met — a direct restricted-party match, a controlled item with no licence, a licence expired at the time of movement. High precision; low false-positive expectation.
Tier 2
Probabilistic. A pattern indicates elevated risk — aggregated ownership crossing 50%, transshipment routing, end-use inconsistency, a new counterparty on controlled goods. Requires judgement; higher review value.
Tiering is honest about certainty. It tells the reviewer whether they are looking at a fact or a signal, and it sets expectations for the false-positive rate the tuning loop tracks per rule.
Provenance. Ta holds no source data of its own — it renders what the platform provides. All feeds are read-only; no data is written back to source systems and none is transmitted to Fortifai servers. This supports self-hosting within Honeywell's authorized boundary.
Three data classes
Honeywell internal
SAP transactions & master data, and the Honeywell-supplied classification and licence masters. Sales orders, deliveries, invoices, routing, customer/vendor master, ECCN/USML status, licence coverage.
Honeywell external / reference
Official screening lists Honeywell is obligated against: OFAC SDN, BIS Entity List, DDTC debarred parties, and EAR Country Group designations. Read for currency; not modified.
D7 source data
The D7 counterparty risk profile: per-regime screening results, UBO / ownership resolution (incl. 50%-rule aggregation), jurisdiction risk scoring, adverse-media, and layered match assessment.

Human-in-the-loop
  • Every anomaly is a persisted case with a lifecycle — never auto-closed.
  • Trade-compliance analysts triage; only humans escalate, dismiss, or flag.
  • Legal disposition is made exclusively by counsel in Privileged Review.
  • Ta suggests next steps; it does not decide.
Attorney–Client Privilege
  • Escalated anomalies enter Privileged Review under attorney–client privilege.
  • Counsel records a disposition, marks privilege (ACP / work product), and adds a legal note.
  • Privileged material is labelled and access-scoped to counsel.
  • Reports carry an explicit boundary statement — evidence, not a compliance conclusion.
Explainability
  • Each anomaly carries a plain-language fusion rationale naming both pillars.
  • The full evidence chain (SAP docs, screening result, ownership, licence) is inspectable.
  • Ask Ta answers "why did this fire?" against the actual case data.
False-positive handling & tuning
  • Confirmed FPs are captured with reason codes (name coincidence, ownership below threshold, licence on file, classification error).
  • FP rate is tracked per rule — the honest counterpart to the tier model.
  • Reason codes feed the rule-tuning loop; no silent suppression.
Audit & screening currency
Every action — triage, escalation, disposition, dismissal, FP flag, share, re-screen — is written to an immutable audit trail with actor, timestamp and detail. Screening is continuous: a new listing or ownership-change event sweeps every open order and contract tied to the affected party, rather than waiting for the next batch.
These limits are stated deliberately. Understanding what the system cannot do is part of using it responsibly.
Data-dependent detection
  • Rules only fire on data that exists — an unrecorded ship-to change or an absent licence-master entry limits coverage.
  • Classification and licence determinations are Honeywell-supplied; Ta does not independently classify items.
  • SAP-native rules do not transfer 1:1 to other ledgers or ERPs without re-derivation.
Signal, not verdict
  • Tier 2 anomalies are probabilistic and expected to include false positives.
  • A composite score ranks review priority; it is not a probability of violation.
  • Ta evidences exposure; it does not establish intent or legal liability.
Non-goals
Ta is not a sanctions-screening engine (it consumes D7's screening), not a customs-filing system, not a licence-management system of record, and not a substitute for a trade-compliance professional or legal counsel. It is a detection-and-case layer that makes existing obligations observable and auditable.
